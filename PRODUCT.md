# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Security researchers, students and reviewers inspecting a photographed or exported QR code. They run the Streamlit app locally, load an image, and need to know two things quickly: whether the printed symbol shows signs of physical alteration, and whether the encoded URL has structural features associated with abuse.

## Product Purpose
Q-TDE (QR Code Tampering & URL Risk Inspection) evaluates a QR image along two independent dimensions: physical integrity (Reed-Solomon syndromes for Version 3-M symbols plus a high-frequency visual residual) and static URL structure (21 weighted rules, no network access). Success is a user who understands both readouts, and what neither of them proves, within seconds of loading an image.

## Brand Personality
Precise, calm, candid. The interface reads like a measurement instrument: it states what was measured, what crossed a threshold, and where its competence ends. No alarmism, no reassurance it cannot back up.

## Anti-references
- Dark neon "cyber" dashboards, shield illustrations, fake terminals.
- SaaS landing templates: three equal feature cards, gradient heroes, hero metrics.
- Any wording that turns "no evidence detected" into "genuine", or a low URL score into "safe".

## Design Principles
1. Two axes, never fused: physical integrity and URL structure are shown side by side and never collapse into one verdict.
2. Say what the check cannot establish, next to the result, not in a footnote.
3. The tool is the hero: the working inspector sits in the first viewport.
4. Numbers are measurements: thresholds, scores and counts are shown with their scale.
5. Quiet by default: color marks state, not decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast in light and dark themes, visible focus on every control, keyboard-operable input, status text announced politely, state never conveyed by color alone (shape and label accompany it), and full support for reduced motion.
